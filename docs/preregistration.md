# Preregistration

**Written before any analysis ran.** This file fixes the hypotheses and the *sign* of the
expected bias. A hypothesis added after seeing output is not a hypothesis; it is a
narrative. `tests/test_naive_must_fail.py` reads the signs from this document's table
(mirrored in code as constants) and fails if the naive estimator lands on the wrong side.

Date written: 2026-10-30. Data: synthetic, generated by `liquidity/generate.py`, seed 42.

## Scope

A classifieds marketplace where the transaction happens off-platform. The platform observes
**views, contacts and removals**. It does **not** observe the sale. Two questions:

- **Q1** — Can liquidity be measured from what the platform sees?
- **Q2** — Does buying visibility create contact demand, or relocate it?

Not in scope: retention modelling, RFM, churn prediction, revenue forecasting, fraud
classification. See `README.md` § Antiscope.

## Definitions fixed before looking

- **Cohort** — listings with `publish_ts` inside the first `COHORT_DAYS` (30) days of the
  data window. Every cohort listing gets a full 30-day observation window; nothing is
  censored by the end of data.
- **`sell_through_true`** — share of the cohort whose sale happened within 30 days of
  publish. Latent. Known only to the generator.
- **`sell_through_proxy`** — share of the cohort whose *visible* removal reason is
  `sold`. Derivable from `data/raw/listings.csv` alone.
- **`contact`** — one distinct `(buyer_id, listing_id)` pair. Repeat contacts by the same
  buyer on the same listing count once.
- **Incrementality** — difference in contacts **per search session** between sessions
  where promoted slots were shown and sessions in the randomized holdout.

## H1 — the proxy is biased, per category

| Statement | Expected sign of `proxy − true` |
|---|---|
| Товары (`goods`) | **positive** — sellers mark "sold" on withdrawal |
| Услуги (`services`) | **negative** — sale closes off-platform, listing lapses |
| Недвижимость (`realty`) | **positive** |
| Автомобили (`auto`) | **negative** |
| Работа (`jobs`) | **positive** |

**H1a (the interesting one):** the platform-level aggregate error is smaller than the
average absolute per-category error. A dashboard that shows one number is not obviously
wrong — it is wrong category by category, in opposite directions, and the errors cancel.
Prediction: `|platform error| < mean(|category error|) / 2`.

**H1b:** a mixture correction on observable signals — `has_replied_contact` and the gap
between last replied contact and removal — recovers `sell_through_true` per category within
±0.03, while the proxy stays ≥0.04 away for at least two categories.

## H2 — paid visibility relocates contacts

- **H2a (naive, expected to fail):** comparing a boosted listing's contacts before and
  after the boost purchase yields a **positive** lift. This is selection, not causality:
  boosts are bought disproportionately by listings with weak organic position.
- **H2b:** the correct contrast — contacts per search session, treated vs randomized
  holdout — is **zero or negative**. Promoted slots push every organic listing down one or
  more positions, and the cumulative loss across the shifted tail exceeds the gain on the
  promoted slot itself.
- **H2c (guardrail):** contacts on **unpaid** listings fall when promoted slots are
  present. `seller_reply_rate` does not rise. Both are reported even when the primary is
  flat, because a primary-only readout would call this a win.

## Decision rules declared in advance

| Outcome | Decision |
|---|---|
| H1a holds and H1b recovers | the platform needs a per-category corrected estimator; a single sell-through number is not reportable |
| H1a fails | the proxy is defensible at platform level; report per-category error only as a caveat |
| H2b ≥ 0 and H2c clean | paid visibility is incremental; raise ad load |
| H2b < 0 | paid visibility is partly a tax on organic reach; price it as such and cap slots per SERP |
| H2c dirty (unpaid contacts fall) | roll back, whatever H2b says |

## What would make this document wrong

If the corrected estimator only works because the generator made the signals separable by
construction, the recovery test measures the generator, not the method. Mitigation is
structural, not rhetorical: the truth file is frozen and never imported by analysis
(`tests/test_no_truth_leak.py`), the recovery bound is ±0.03 rather than exact, and the
naive estimator's failure is asserted on a *pre-declared sign* it cannot be tuned toward.
